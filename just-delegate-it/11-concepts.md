# 11 Rules of AI Delegation — Explained Simply

---

## Rule 1
### Asking gets an answer. Giving a job gets a result.

> "Asking AI gets you an answer. Giving AI a job gets you a result you can check."

- **Asking** → `"What is SEO?"` → AI gives you information
- **Giving a job** → `"Write an SEO audit report for this page"` → AI gives you something you can review, verify, and use

**Key shift:** Move from *chatting with AI* to *managing AI like a worker.*

---

## Rule 2
### Describe the result — not the steps.

> "Describe the result you want, and leave the steps to AI."

- **Wrong:** *"First search the web, then summarize, then format it as a table..."*
- **Right:** *"Give me a comparison table of the top 5 AI coding tools."*

You own the **outcome.** AI owns the **process.**

---

## Rule 3
### Answer six questions before AI starts.

> "A Delegation Brief answers six questions before AI starts, so that you have something to check the result against."

The brief is your **benchmark** — without it, you have no standard to measure the output against.

| Question | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| Outcome | What should exist when done? |
| Context | What must AI know upfront? |
| Constraints | What rules apply? |
| Authority | What can AI decide alone? |
| Deliverable | What form should the result take? |
| Verification | What will you check before accepting? |

---

## Rule 4
### When the job needs current facts — make AI search, then check that it did.

> "When the job needs current facts, make AI search, and check that it did."

- AI's training data has a **cutoff date**
- For anything recent — news, prices, stats, events — AI must **search the web**
- Your job: confirm it actually searched, and that the sources are real

**Don't assume. Verify the search happened.**

---

## Rule 5
### When you have the source — give it, set the boundary, then check it stayed inside.

> "When the job needs a source only you have, give it, tell AI to stay inside it, and check that it did."

- Upload your document, data, or file
- Tell AI: *"Use only this source. Do not add outside information."*
- After: check that AI did not drift outside your source and invent things

**Your source = your boundary. Enforce it.**

---

## Rule 6
### Ask for the finished thing — and check every "unverified" mark survived.

> "Ask for the finished thing, a table or a document, and check that every 'unverified' mark survived."

- Ask for the actual deliverable — a **table, document, report, file**
- If AI was asked to flag uncertain claims with a mark like `[unverified]`, check they are still there
- AI should not silently remove uncertainty markers to look more confident

**Completed form + honest uncertainty = a usable result.**

---

## Rule 7
### Tell AI what it may decide — then read the plan before work starts.

> "Say what AI may decide alone and what it must ask about, and read the plan before the work starts."

- **Authority** tells AI the boundary between its decisions and yours
- **Plan-first** means: ask AI to list its steps, then you approve before it continues

```
You: "List your steps and wait for my go-ahead."
AI:  "Here's what I plan to do: ..."
You: "Approved. Begin." (or "Change step 2 first.")
```

**You see the map before the journey starts.**

---

## Rule 8
### Save what doesn't change — put it where AI reads it automatically.

> "When a job comes back, save what does not change, and put it into a Project where AI reads it by itself."

- Repeating the same context every chat wastes time
- Move **stable instructions, tone rules, background info** into a **Project**
- AI reads it automatically in every new chat — you don't have to repeat yourself

**Once written → always applied.**

---

## Rule 9
### When AI drifts — use the smallest fix, then update the brief.

> "When AI drifts, fix it with the smallest message that works, then write the lesson into the brief."

- **Drift** = AI goes off tone, off topic, or off format
- Fix it with **one focused correction** — not a rewrite of everything
- Then **update your brief** so the same drift does not happen next time

```
Drift → Minimal correction → Brief updated → Problem prevented
```

**Every intervention is a lesson. Capture it.**

---

## Rule 10
### Verification has four steps — and the responsibility is always yours.

> "Verification has four steps, and the accountability for the result stays with you."

The four steps inside Verify:

```
Identify → Trace → Challenge → Decide
```

| Step | What You Do |
|------|-------------|
| **Identify** | Find the claims that need checking |
| **Trace** | Find where they came from |
| **Challenge** | Test whether they hold up |
| **Decide** | Accept, Correct, Investigate, or Reject |

> AI produced it. **You are responsible for it.**  
> If it goes out with your name on it — you own it.

---

## Rule 11
### A good brief works in any AI tool — because it describes the job, not the tool.

> "A good brief runs in a second AI without a rewrite, because it describes the job and not the tool."

- A bad brief: *"Use the web search button, then click generate..."* — tool-specific, breaks elsewhere
- A good brief: *"Research and summarize the top 3 competitors in X market using current sources"* — works anywhere

**The job is portable. The tool is not.**
