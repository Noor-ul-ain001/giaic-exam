# Concept 1: From Asking AI to Giving AI the Job

---

## The Core Idea

Most people start with AI by **asking questions.**
This concept teaches you to make a shift — from *asking* to *delegating.*

---

## The Difference: Side by Side

| | **Asking** | **Giving a Job** |
|---|---|---|
| **What you type** | `What are some good courses for learning AI agents?` | `Give me a table of three courses for learning AI agents, with a link and one-line description for each.` |
| **What you get** | A list of suggestions | A table you can hand to someone |
| **Who does the next step** | You — open each link, compare, decide | AI — it already did the first pass |
| **What is left for you** | All the work | Just the checking |
| **Did AI guess?** | Yes — it guessed what form you wanted | No — you told it exactly what to produce |

---

## What Actually Changed?

The job sentence is slightly longer — but **length is not the difference.**

The difference is that it asked for **a thing to exist:**
- Three courses
- In a table
- With links

> That is delegating. You gave AI the **responsibility** for producing the result — not just answering your question.

This does not require a paid plan or a special model.
**It depends on what kind of sentence you type.**

---

## Do It Now

**Step 1:** Open a fresh chat (new conversation, clean slate).

**Step 2:** Type this question:
```
What are some good courses for learning AI agents?
```

**Step 3:** Put that answer next to the table you got from the opener.

**Step 4:** Ask yourself:

- Which answer is something you could **hand to someone else?**
- Which is **advice you would still have to act on?**
- Did AI **separate facts from advice?**
- Which answer left you with something you could **check?**

---

## Verify Before You Accept

Open **one link** from the table. Write one line recording what you found:

| Outcome | Example |
|---------|---------|
| **Agreed** | The page matched what the table said |
| **Disagreed on a detail** | The course title was different / price changed |
| **Link did not work** | Page not found, or led somewhere else |

> Keep this line. You will do this at every concept.
> By Concept 10 you will know **exactly what is worth checking** and how.

---

## Your Turn — The Running Job

Replace `"AI agents"` with **something you want to learn,** in the language you think best in:

```
Give me a table of three courses for learning [your subject],
with a link and one-line description for each.
```

**Examples of subjects:**
- Accounting
- Excel
- Graphic design
- Project management
- Spoken English
- Public speaking
- Python

> Do not research it yourself first. **Give AI the job.**

---

## The Running Job — What It Is

This chat is your **running job** — one task you carry through the whole course.

At each concept, you come back and **add one thing** to it.
By Concept 10, you will have written a complete Delegation Brief — without ever filling in a form.

> If your subject has no courses with clear pages to verify,
> use **"learning Python from zero"** as a fallback.
> It has many free courses with checkable pages.

---

## Check Your Work

Before moving to the next concept, confirm all three:

- [ ] Did I ask for **a thing to exist**, not just for advice?
- [ ] Did AI do **work I would otherwise have done myself?**
- [ ] Did I open **at least one link** and compare the page with the table?

> If any answer is **no** — change the message and run it again before continuing.

---

## Remember

> **Asking** gets you an answer — and keeps the work with you.
> **Giving AI a job** gets you a result — and leaves you only the checking.

**Check yourself:**
*What does a question get you, and what does a job get you?*
