# Concept 3: Write the Delegation Brief

---

## The Core Idea

You have already been writing a brief — just without labels.
This concept names the six parts and shows you what each one does.

---

## The Six Lines — Explained

### The Brief (AI Agents Example)

```
Outcome:      Three free online courses for learning AI agents, compared,
              with one recommended for a complete beginner.

Context:      I have never built or used an AI agent, and I do not know
              how to program. I can study about five hours a week.

Constraints:  Only courses that can be started for free, and only courses
              that do not expect programming knowledge. Search for current
              information rather than answering from what you already know.

Authority:    Choose the sources yourself. If two courses are equal, pick
              the shorter one without asking me. Say clearly if a course
              is paid, or if it expects me to know how to program.

Deliverable:  A short table with provider, language, time needed, whether
              a certificate costs money, and a link. Then a two-sentence
              recommendation.

Verification: Mark anything you could not confirm as "unverified"
              instead of guessing.
```

---

## What Each Line Does

| Line | Question It Answers | Why It Matters |
|------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Outcome** | What should exist when the work is done? | Sets the standard you judge the result against |
| **Context** | What does AI need to know that it cannot know on its own? | Fills the gap between AI's knowledge and your situation |
| **Constraints** | What requirements and limits apply? | Prevents AI from giving you something technically correct but useless |
| **Authority** | What may AI decide without asking you? | Makes hidden decisions visible — AI decides anyway; this controls what |
| **Deliverable** | What form should the result take? | Shapes the output so someone can actually use it |
| **Verification** | What will you check before accepting? | Turns reading the result into checking it |

---

## The Three Lines You Already Knew

From earlier concepts, you had already written three of these — just without labels:

- **Outcome** → the sentence from Concept 2: *"When this is done, I will have..."*
- **Constraints** → "free to start" was already a limit you cared about
- **Deliverable** → "a table with links" was already your required form

---

## The Three Lines People Skip

### Context
What AI cannot know about you unless you say it.

```
Easy to write. Just ask: What would a stranger need to know
to do this job well for me?
```

### Authority *(most skipped)*
The decisions you let AI make alone.

> Without this line, AI decides for you — **silently** — and you never learn a decision was made.

```
"Pick the shorter one without asking me."     ← a decision you gave AI
"Say clearly if a course is paid."            ← a limit on that decision
```

### Verification *(most ignored)*
Written **before** the work starts — not after.

> Writing it first turns **reading** the result into **checking** it.
> You name what matters enough to open a source for, while you still know why it matters.

## Do It Now

**Step 1:** Rewrite your running job using all six labeled lines.

**Step 2:** Run it in a fresh chat.

**Step 3:** Put the result next to your Concept 1 table.

**Step 4:** Ask yourself:

- Did AI keep to the **deliverable** line, column for column?
- Did it **mark anything as unverified?**
  - If nothing was marked — is that because everything was confirmed, or because the line was ignored?
  - Open one link and find out.
- Did it say whether the recommended course is **paid**, or **expects programming** you do not have?

---

## Verify Before You Accept

Take **one item** the result marked as `unverified` and check it yourself.

If **nothing was marked**, open the recommended course page and check:
- Its **price**
- What it **expects you to already know**

Write one line recording:

```
What the brief asked to be checked → What you checked → What you found
```

## Your Turn

Write a six-line brief for **a real job you need done this week** — not a learning job.

**Examples:**
- Two phones you are choosing between
- A long message thread you need summarized
- A letter you need drafted

**Rules:**
- Six lines, one per question
- If a line does not apply, write `none` — do not delete the line
- Remove any names or private information before pasting

---

## Check Your Work

Before moving on, confirm your brief answers all six:

- [ ] What **outcome** should exist?
- [ ] What **context** does AI need that it cannot know on its own?
- [ ] What **constraints** matter?
- [ ] What may AI **decide without asking?**
- [ ] What **deliverable** should it produce, and in what form?
- [ ] What will you **check before accepting** the result?

> If a question matters for this job and the brief leaves it unanswered — **fix the brief before continuing.**

---

## A Note on What This Is

> This is a **thinking tool, not a form.**
> Nobody fills in six boxes for a two-line job.

But every time a result disappoints you, **one of these six questions was unanswered.**
Now you can say which one.

---

## Where Each Line Came From

```
Concept 1 → You described a result         = Outcome
Concept 2 → You named limits               = Constraints + Deliverable
Concept 3 → You added what was missing     = Context + Authority + Verification
```

The brief did not arrive all at once.
**You built it one concept at a time — without ever filling in a form.**

## Remember
