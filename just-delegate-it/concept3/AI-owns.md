# Concept 7: Decide How Much of the Job AI Owns

---

## The Core Idea

**Authority** has two faces — and most people only see one.

| Face | What It Means |
|------|--------------|
| **Face 1** | How much of the **method** AI decides |
| **Face 2** | What AI may **do or touch** without asking you |

Both matter. This concept teaches you to write both — clearly, before the job starts.

---

## The Two Faces of Authority

### Face 1 — The Method
> How much of the plan does AI own?

In Concept 3, Authority was one line.
Here you go deeper: you ask AI to **show you the plan first** and wait for your approval.

### Face 2 — The Actions
> What may AI actually do without asking?

In a chat window today, this means **decisions.**
Later, when AI can act in the world, this becomes **permissions.**

> Write the lines now, in the form you will need later.
> The habit becomes the permission.

---

## Do It Now

Open a fresh chat. Run your running brief with **one instruction added at the top:**

```
Before you start, list the steps you plan to take, in order,
and wait for my go-ahead. Do not begin until I say go.
```

**Then:**
1. Read the plan AI gives you
2. **Change one step** — add a source, remove one, or change the order
3. Type `go`

> This is **plan-first** — the cheapest moment to fix a mistake is before the job runs.

---

## What Just Changed?

### The Cheapest Moment in Any Job

```
Before the run  →  costs a minute to read, a sentence to fix
After the run   →  costs the entire run
```

> A plan costs a minute to read and a sentence to fix.
> The same mistake, found after the run, costs the run.

### Plan-First = Observe + Intervene at Their Cheapest

| Loop Step | How Plan-First Serves It |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Observe** | You see the method before it runs |
| **Intervene** | You redirect before anything is produced |

### An Approved Plan Is Not a Cage

> AI may find a better route once it starts — and a good agent often does.

What it may **not** do:
- Cross a line you drew
- Change something that matters **without telling you**

> The failure is the **silent change** — not the change itself.

---

## The Four Boundary Lines

These are the limits that protect you when AI can act in the world:

| Boundary | Why It Exists |
|----------|--------------|
| **Research, but do not purchase** | Money that moves is hard to move back |
| **Draft, but do not send** | Sending creates a promise someone else can see |
| **Inspect, but do not delete** | Deleting destroys the evidence you need to check the work |
| **Choose, but say when you chose** | A silent decision is one you cannot review |

> Write these now — even in a chat where AI cannot act yet.
> The same lines become **permission modes** the day AI can act.

---

## How Trust Grows Over Time

```
First run   →  Read the plan AND every step
Second run  →  Read the plan AND every step
Third run   →  Read the plan AND every step
...
Fifth clean run  →  Read the plan
```

> Trust grows with a **record of runs you have checked.**
> You do not give more authority because AI seems confident.
> You give more because you have **verified it earned it.**

---

## What to Notice

After running your brief with plan-first, ask:

- Did the plan include a step **you would not have chosen?** Was it better or worse?
- Did AI **wait for "go"** — or start anyway?
  - Starting anyway is an **authority failure in miniature**
- Which **boundary line** did your job actually need?

---

## Verify Before You Accept

Put the **plan you approved** beside **what AI actually did.**

> Every difference is one question to ask it.

| What You Find | What It Means |
|---------------|--------------|
| Plan followed exactly | Good — authority was respected |
| Small deviation, AI told you | Acceptable — transparent change |
| Change made silently | Authority failure — this is what the concept exists to catch |

```
Authority: [one thing AI may decide alone]
```

## Optional: Authority with a Real Action

> Not required for this course. Everything so far happened inside a chat.
> This exercise lets AI act in the world once — so the Authority line has something real to hold.

**What you need:** A Netlify account (free) + a Netlify connector in your assistant.

**The brief:**
```
Outcome:      A one-page website about what I do and how to reach me.

Context:      [What you do, in three lines, and where you are based.]

Constraints:  No phone number. Use an email I am happy to make public,
              or a contact form. Nothing I would not put on a poster.

Authority:    Build the page and show it to me first. Do not create
              or publish anything on Netlify until I say go.
              Use the site name I give you.

Deliverable:  One HTML page, live at a Netlify address.

Verification: I will open the address, check that it loads, that
              nothing private is on it, and that the contact method works.
```

**What to watch for:**

| What Happens | What It Means |
|-------------|--------------|
| AI builds and **waits for go** | Authority respected — correct behavior |
| AI **publishes before go** | Authority failure — you have seen it for real |

> If you want the page gone after — **delete it yourself** in your Netlify account.
> `Inspect, but do not delete` — that one stays with you.

## The Running Brief — What It Now Contains

```
Concept 1  →  Outcome
Concept 2  →  Outcome + Constraints
Concept 3  →  All six lines
Concept 4  →  Constraints: current information required
Concept 5  →  Context: source added, stay-inside instruction
Concept 6  →  Deliverable: form named precisely, caveats protected
Concept 7  →  Authority: method veto + action boundaries written
              Plan-first added to every run
```
