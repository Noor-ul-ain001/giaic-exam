## The Four Steps of Verification

### Step 1 — Identify
**Which claims matter enough to check?**

Not everything. Focus where an error would cost you:

| Check These | Examples |
|-------------|---------|
| **Prices** | Course fee, subscription cost |
| **Dates** | Start dates, enrollment deadlines |
| **Figures and calculations** | Hours per week, totals |
| **Quotations** | Exact words attributed to a source |
| **Recommendations** | The one you will act on |
| **Legal or safety claims** | Anything with consequences |
| **Anything hard to reverse** | A purchase, a send, a delete |

> Spend your checking where an **error would cost you.**

---

### Step 2 — Trace
**Follow one claim to its source.**

| Action | What It Means |
|--------|--------------|
| Open the source | Go to the actual page — not a summary of it |
| Find the sentence | The exact sentence that supports the claim |
| Check the date | When was this page last updated? |
| Ask: is this source good enough? | Provider's own page beats a blog about the provider |

> A citation is not verification.
> **Checking the citation is verification.**

---

### Step 3 — Challenge
**Ask how the answer could be wrong.**

| Question | Why It Matters |
|----------|---------------|
| Could this have changed since the page was written? | Pages go stale |
| Is AI repeating a secondary source? | The original may say something different |
| Is an assumption being stated as a fact? | Confidence is not correctness |
| Do two credible sources disagree? | The primary source usually wins — unless it is the older one |

---

### Step 4 — Decide
**Verification ends with a human decision. There are four:**

| Decision | What It Means | What You Do Next |
|----------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Accept** | The evidence is enough | Use the result |
| **Correct** | A specific error can be fixed | Go back to Intervene |
| **Investigate** | Evidence is incomplete or contradictory | Trace again |
| **Reject** | Result is not reliable enough to use | Take the job back |

```
Accept      →  use it
Correct     →  fix it (back to Intervene)
Investigate →  trace again (back to Trace)
Reject      →  take the job back
```

## Do It Now

**Step 1:** Take your latest running-job result.

**Step 2:** Before you open anything — write down **two claims that matter most:**
- The price
- The language
- Whether the course still runs

**Step 3:** For **one of them**, do all four steps: Identify, Trace, Challenge, Decide.

**Step 4:** End with **one of the four decisions** and write one sentence explaining why.

---

## The One Rule Under All Four Steps

> AI doing the work does **not** transfer accountability to AI.
> **The name on the result is yours.**

This does not change with better models.
It does not change when the job ran automatically.
It does not change when the result looks right.

---

## What to Notice

After working through the four steps, ask:

- Which two claims did you pick — and **why those rather than the others?**
- Did the source **support the claim** — or only resemble it?
- Which of the four decisions did you reach — and can you **say why in one sentence?**
