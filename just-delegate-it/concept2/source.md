# Concept 5: Give AI the Source

---

## The Core Idea

Some jobs need information **AI cannot search for** — because only you have it.

- A syllabus you downloaded
- A notice from your office
- A message thread
- A policy page
- A course description you saved

You have it. AI does not. **You give it.**

---

## Before You Paste Anything — Privacy First

> A free assistant is **not your filing cabinet.**

Before pasting any real content, remove:

| Remove | Examples |
|--------|---------|
| **Names** | People's full names, usernames |
| **Phone numbers** | Any contact numbers |
| **Account numbers** | IDs, reference numbers |
| **Anything private** | What you would not pin to a public notice board |

### If the Job Holds Something Sensitive

Both major assistants offer a **chat that is not saved** to your history and does not add to memory:

| Assistant | Name for It |
|-----------|------------|
| **ChatGPT** | Temporary Chat |
| **Claude** | Incognito Chat |

> **Important:** Neither makes a paste disappear.
> The provider still holds a copy for a time.
> **The first rule still stands — remove what is private.**

For the bigger question of whether a piece of work or data may go into AI at all, see: **Governance, Risk & Responsible Use** — the last course in Foundations.

## Do It Now

**Step 1:** Open the page of the course AI recommended. Copy the text that describes it — a page or two at most.

**Step 2:** Open a fresh chat and paste this:

```
Using only the text below, answer three questions:
1. What will I be able to do at the end?
2. How many hours a week does it expect?
3. Is any previous knowledge required?

For each answer, quote the sentence you used.
If the text does not say, write "not in the source".

[paste the text here]
```

**Step 3:** Read the result for the three things that matter — covered below.

---

## The Two Instructions That Make a Source a Source

> Without them, AI mixes **what you gave it** with **what it remembers** — and you cannot tell which is which.

| Instruction | What It Does |
|-------------|-------------|
| `"Using only the text below"` | Locks AI inside your source — no outside knowledge |
| `"Quote the sentence you used"` | Makes every answer point back to something you can find |

With both instructions in place:

- Every answer has a sentence you can locate
- `"not in the source"` becomes a **good answer** — not a gap AI fills for you

---

## Paste vs. Attach — Which to Use

| Situation | What to Do |
|-----------|-----------|
| **A page or two of text** | Paste it directly after your instructions |
| **A longer document** | Attach the file using the **plus (+) or paperclip** beside the message box |

> Either way, the job is the same:
> **Give AI the source. Tell it to stay inside. Check that it did.**

---

## What to Notice

After running the prompt, ask:

- Did **every answer** come with a quoted sentence — or did some arrive without one?
- Did it write `"not in the source"` anywhere — or did it answer everything confidently?
- Did it **add anything** the text did not say?

---

## Verify Before You Accept

Take **one quoted sentence** and find it in the text you pasted.

| What You Find | What It Means |
|---------------|--------------|
| Word for word match | Good — AI quoted correctly |
| Close but not exact | AI paraphrased and called it a quote |
| Not there at all | AI invented it |

**Write down which one you found.**

> This is the difference between a citation and verification.
> **You checked. That is verification.**
