# Concept 4: Give AI Something to Research

---

## The Core Idea

AI has **two ways of knowing things.** This concept teaches you to tell them apart — and to make sure AI uses the right one.

---

## The Two Ways AI Knows Things

| | **Remembering** | **Searching** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Knowledge from training data | Looking something up right now |
| **How current** | Out of date — has a cutoff | Current — today's facts |
| **Comes with** | No source — just sounds sure | A link or page you can open |
| **Good for** | Concepts, definitions, history | Prices, dates, links, availability |
| **Risk** | Confident but wrong or stale | Source may be old or moved |

> A fact that came from **remembering** arrives alone — and sounds just as sure as one that came from searching.

---

## Do It Now

Open a fresh chat. Run your six-line brief with **one line added at the end:**

```
For each course, say whether you found it by searching just now
or already knew about it, and give the date of the page you used.
```

Read the answer for **two words:** `searched` and `knew`.

| Word | What It Tells You |
|------|------------------|
| `searched` | AI looked it up — check the source and its date |
| `knew` | AI answered from memory — this is the fact to check first |

> The word is a **hint.** The source and its date are the **evidence.**

---

## Two Important Cautions

### 1. No source ≠ AI searched
> If a current claim arrives with **no source**, do not assume AI looked — whatever its answer says about itself.

### 2. A source is not a date
> Pages go stale. When it matters that a fact is **current**, find the date on the page yourself.

```
Citation ≠ Verification
Checking the citation = Verification
```

---

## Why Your Opener Link May Have Been Stale

If a link from Concept 1 did not work, one of two things happened:

- AI answered from **memory** instead of searching
- AI searched but used a **page that had since moved or changed**

Both are solved by the same habit: **check the date on the page itself.**

## The Web Search Control

Some assistants show a **web search switch.**

| Mode | When to Use It |
|------|---------------|
| **Web search on** | Job needs today's facts — prices, dates, availability, links |
| **Deep research mode** | Job's deliverable *is* a report — reads many pages before writing |
| **Web search off** | Job needs concepts, reasoning, drafting — not current data |

> What each plan includes **changes often.**
> Check the **plans page** rather than relying on this course.

## What to Notice

After running your brief with the added line, ask:

- Did each course come with **a source you can open?**
- Did any current claim arrive **with no source?**
- Did it say `knew` about anything? → **Check that fact first.**
- Did the answer **change from your Concept 3 run?**
  - If yes — was the world different, or was the search?

---

## Verify Before You Accept

**Step 1:** Choose one fact that came with a page link.

**Step 2:** Open the page.

**Step 3:** Find the **exact sentence** that supports the fact.

**Step 4:** Find the **date** on the page.

Then ask:

| Question | What It Means If No |
|----------|-------------------|
| Is the sentence there? | The link was decoration |
| Is the date recent? | The fact may be stale |

> A citation is not verification.
> **Checking the citation is verification.**
