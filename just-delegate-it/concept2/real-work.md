## What Part 1 Actually Was

You ran the same rhythm three times in Part 1 — and never stopped to name it.

That rhythm has a name: **The Delegation Loop.**

---

## The Delegation Loop — All Six Steps

```
Define → Delegate → Observe → Intervene → Verify → Accept
  ↑                                                    |
  └─────────────── (repeat if rejected) ───────────────┘
```

| Step | What It Means | Where You Did It in Part 1 |
|------|--------------|---------------------------|
| **Define** | Say what result you want | The one-sentence outcome you wrote in Concept 2 |
| **Delegate** | Give AI responsibility for producing it | Every message that asked for a thing to exist |
| **Observe** | Let AI do the routine work without directing every step | Reading the table instead of building it yourself |
| **Intervene** | Correct, redirect, constrain, or clarify when AI drifts | When AI refused the recipe and you told it to search instead |
| **Verify** | Check the claims, numbers, and actions that matter | Every link you opened |
| **Accept** | Decide: fit for use, or send it back? | The decision you made at the end of each concept |

---

## Who Owns What

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│   YOU        →   AI does the work   →   YOU                 │
│  Define           Observe                Verify             │
│  Delegate         (Intervene if          Accept             │
│                    needed)                                  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> **AI owns the middle.**
> **You own both ends.**

- **What the job is** — only you can answer that
- **Whether the result is fit for use** — only you can decide that

AI can **help** you with any of the four steps you own.
It cannot **take** them.

---

## The Rule for Part 2: Need First, Then Tool

> A control is learned **when a job needs it** — not before.

Every concept in Part 2 adds **one thing the job needs**, and with it **one control** on the screen that answers that need.

You do not learn buttons in advance.
**The job teaches you the tool.**

## Two Terms to Know Before Part 2 Begins

These are taught fully in Concept 8 — but you will see them before then, so here is one line on each:

### Project
> A space in an assistant that holds your **files and standing instructions**, so that every chat inside it starts already informed.

### Knowledge
> The **files a Project holds** — what AI may read in every chat inside that Project.

## The Shape of Part 2

Each concept in Part 2 follows the same pattern:

```
Running job needs something new
        ↓
One concept teaches how to give it that
        ↓
One control on the screen answers the need
        ↓
Brief grows by one line
        ↓
Loop runs again
```

By the end of Part 2, your running job will have **real material** behind it — not just a sentence, but sources, constraints, a deliverable, and a verification standard.
