# Concept 6: Ask for the Deliverable, Not an Answer

---

## The Core Idea

Every job so far ended in a **chat message.**
A real job ends in **a thing someone can use.**

---

## Answer vs. Deliverable

| | **Answer** | **Deliverable** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | A reply in the chat | A shaped, usable result |
| **Who it is for** | You, right now | Anyone — saved, sent, handed on |
| **Where it lives** | Inside the conversation | Outside it — a file, table, document |
| **What happens to caveats** | Usually survive | Often silently dropped |
| **Example** | A paragraph about courses | A one-page document with table + sources |

---

## Do It Now

In the chat with your latest running-job result, type:

```
Turn this into a one-page document I could send to a friend who asked
me the same question: a two-sentence introduction, the comparison
table, the recommendation, and the sources. Make it a file I can
download if you can. Keep every "unverified" mark exactly where it is.
```

---

## What Just Changed?

The **Deliverable line** of your brief has done its work.

- Your opener table was already a small deliverable
- Now the result must **leave the chat** — as a document someone can open, save, and read without ever seeing the conversation

---

## Where the Deliverable Appears

This depends on the assistant you are using:

| What You See | What It Means |
|-------------|--------------|
| A **panel beside the chat** | Work product separated from conversation |
| A **downloadable file** | Same move — result is now portable |

> Both do the same thing: **separate the work product from the conversation about it.**

When the answer is data — ask for a **table or spreadsheet**, not paragraphs.

---

## Why the Last Line Matters Most

> `"Keep every 'unverified' mark exactly where it is."`

This line looks minor. It is not.

**Deliverables lose caveats.**

An `unverified` mark that survived three chat replies can **vanish** when the same content is poured into a tidy document — because tidy is what the document is for.

```
Chat answer:    "The course is free [unverified]."
Document:       "The course is free."
```

> The caveat disappeared. The document looks more confident than the evidence warrants.
> **You have to explicitly protect your uncertainty markers.**

---

## What to Notice

After producing the document, ask:

- Does the document match the **deliverable line column for column?**
- Is **every `unverified` mark** from the chat still in the document?
- Could someone **read the document without ever seeing the chat?**

---

## Verify Before You Accept

**Count the caveats** in two places:

| Where | What to Count |
|-------|--------------|
| **Chat answer** | Every `unverified`, every `may`, every `check` |
| **Document** | The same words |

> If the second number is **smaller** — find what was dropped and put it back.
